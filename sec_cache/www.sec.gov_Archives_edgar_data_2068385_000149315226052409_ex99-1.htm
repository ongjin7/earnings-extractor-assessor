Sharon AI Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results

 

Total Contract Value1 of ~$9.6bn over 5.5 years2,

including $2.5bn contracted since the second quarter of 2026

 

ARR3 of $50m in the third quarter of 2026, anticipated to increase to $400-$450m in January 2027 as the Company continues to deploy successfully

 

212MW of total capacity expected in 2027 across Australia and New Zealand, including ~155MW4 expected to be available for sale into the high-demand, short and long-term GPU compute market

 

NEW YORK, November 16, 2026 – SharonAI Holdings Inc. (NASDAQ: SHAZ) and its subsidiaries (“Sharon AI” or “the Company”), a leading Australian Neocloud, today reported its financial and operational results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026.

 

All amounts are in U.S. dollars unless otherwise indicated.

 

Third Quarter 2026 Highlights

 

●Execution and Delivery

 

○Power onsite at all data center sites with contracted customers in Australia and New Zealand, with no reliance on future utility grid connections or upgrades, removing a key external dependency in bringing contracted capacity and customer billing online
○~1,000 NVIDIA B300 GPU cluster delivered and accepted by a global technology company

 

●

Customer Momentum

 

○$1.32bn Total Contract Value (“TCV”)1, five-year, take-or-pay contract with a global AI lab

 

●Capacity and Platform

 

○Added 80MW of incremental capacity in Australia, bringing expected total capacity to 212MW in 2027, further supported by a growing pipeline of additional capacity in 2028
○Five-year Rafay Systems platform agreement to orchestrate up to 150,000 GPUs, and collaboration with VAST Data on the launch of DataEnclave, bringing leading AI models to customer-controlled infrastructure

 

●

Balance Sheet and Capital

 

○Cash and cash equivalents of $1.3bn at September 30, 2026
○Property and equipment (net) of $1.7bn at September 30, 2026

 

●Leadership and Governance

 

○Appointed Anuj Goel, ex-Macquarie, as Chief Financial Officer
○Appointed Melissa Anastasiou, ex-Spark New Zealand, as Chief Legal Officer
○Appointed David Burns, ex-Telstra, as Chief Operating Officer, with Co-founder Andrew Leece transitioning to Head of Strategic Partnerships

 

 

 

 

 

Third Quarter 2026 Financial Results

 

●Revenue: $9.9m, an increase of $9.4m or 1,859% from third quarter 2025 and 414% from second quarter 2026
●Net income (loss): $(43.8m), compared to a net loss of $(1.6m) in third quarter 2025
●Adjusted EBITDA5: $(25.8m), compared to $(1.1m) in third quarter 2025

 

Subsequent Highlights

 

In the fourth quarter 2026 to-date, the Company has demonstrated continued business momentum:

 

●~30MW of 129MW contracted capacity expected to be billing from January 2027, attributable to $400-450m ARR3
●~$1.0bn TCV1, five-year, take-or-pay contract with a global services company
●$170m TCV1, four-year, take-or-pay contract with a leading US-based AI inference platform
●Data center energization achieved for ~8,000 NVIDIA B300 and ~4,000 NVIDIA GB300 clusters, GPU compute now undergoing final commissioning, testing and NVIDIA Infrastructure Services (NVIS) verification ahead of expected customer billing
●Over $2.6bn of institutional debt and equity capital secured since December 20256 plus customer deposits of ~US$0.8bn (as we deploy7)
●$356m inaugural fixed rate, committed, senior secured, GPU-backed debt facility with Goldman Sachs and select large private credit funds, the first in an expected series of GPU-backed financings
●Additional GPU-backed debt facilities well advanced, supported by the majority of Sharon AI’s TCV from customers with strong investment-grade credit ratings
●Guy Wylie, formerly Head of Corporate Finance and Treasurer of Telstra, appointed as Head of Capital and Strategy

 

Management Commentary

 

“Since December 2025, Sharon AI has secured approximately $2.6 billion of capital, including our inaugural GPU-backed facility valued at $356m, establishing a repeatable financing structure supported by customer contracts,” said Anuj Goel, Chief Financial Officer of Sharon AI. “Further GPU-backed facilities to fund the next stage of the build-out are well advanced. The market remains open and highly interested in our secured GPU-backed debt which we intend to raise against signed take-or-pay contracts, secured against the GPUs dedicated to them. The majority of our TCV1 is with customers that hold investment-grade credit ratings, and we expect greater than 75% of our aggregate secured debt packages to stem from the NVIDIA AI Compute Program.”

 

“In the third quarter, we advanced the conversion of our contracted commitments into live AI infrastructure at scale, delivering and achieving customer acceptance of the initial deployment for our global technology customer,” said James Manning, Co-founder and Chief Executive Officer of Sharon AI. “We also achieved SOC 2 Type 2 attestation, an important security and compliance milestone.

 

 

 

 

 

“Globally, demand for AI compute is outpacing the power available to deliver it, and this is where Australia, and Sharon AI, stand apart. Our TCV1 has increased to ~$9.6 billion over 5.5 years2, with this TCV only including the floor price of the NVIDIA AI Compute Program for ~40,000 NVIDIA GB300s online in 2027.

 

“Over the course of 2026 we have seen pricing on our long-term contracts strengthen from ~$15 million to more than $19 million annualized revenue per MW, with new customer prepayments at up to 30% of TCV or 60% of capex. In relation to the NVIDIA AI Compute Program, we expect to sign shorter-duration contracts at over $30 million annualized revenue per MW.

 

“With power in place, we are executing on delivery alongside our deployment partners, and that is our focus in the fourth quarter and beyond. We expect to complete commissioning, testing and NVIS verification of the ESDS and global technology customer deployments in December 2026 and January 2027, taking ARR3 from approximately $50 million for September 2026 to an anticipated $400-450m ARR3 for January 2027, with our additional deployments, including the NVIDIA AI Compute Program, following through 2027. With a strong long-term contracted customer base, live deployments and ~155MW4 of capacity available for sale, strengthening short and long-term GPU pricing and access to institutional capital, we are building a leading sovereign AI compute platform at increasing scale across Australia and New Zealand.”

Non-GAAP Financial Measures

 

This press release includes “Adjusted EBITDA,” which is a non-GAAP financial measure. The Company defines Adjusted EBITDA as net income (loss) adjusted to exclude: (i) interest expense (income), net; (ii) income tax expense (benefit); (iii) depreciation and amortization; (iv) stock-based compensation expense; (v) fair value adjustments on convertible notes; and (vi) other non-cash or non-recurring items that management does not consider indicative of the Company’s ongoing operating performance. Adjusted EBITDA is not a substitute for net income (loss) or any other measure of financial performance prepared in accordance with U.S. GAAP and may not be comparable to similarly titled measures used by other companies. Management believes Adjusted EBITDA is useful to investors because it provides a supplemental measure of the Company’s core operating performance by excluding the effects of capital structure decisions (such as interest expense and fair value changes on convertible notes), non-cash charges (such as depreciation, amortization and stock-based compensation), and tax impacts that can vary significantly between periods and across companies. Management uses Adjusted EBITDA to evaluate the Company’s performance, compare performance across periods, and assist in the allocation of resources. Investors are cautioned that Adjusted EBITDA has limitations as an analytical tool and should not be considered in isolation or as a substitute for analysis of the Company’s results as reported under U.S. GAAP.

 

A reconciliation of Adjusted EBITDA to the most directly comparable U.S. GAAP financial measure is included in the tables accompanying this press release. To the extent the Company provides forward-looking Adjusted EBITDA guidance in connection with this release or the related earnings call, a reconciliation of such forward-looking non-GAAP measure to the most directly comparable U.S. GAAP measure may not be available without unreasonable effort due to the inherent difficulty in forecasting and quantifying certain amounts, including but not limited to fair value adjustments on convertible notes, stock-based compensation expense, and other non-cash or non-recurring items, the timing and magnitude of which may be significant.
