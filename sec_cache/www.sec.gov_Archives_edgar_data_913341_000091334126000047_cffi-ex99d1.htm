C&F Financial Corporation

Announces Net Income for Third Quarter and First Nine Months of 2026

​

Toano, Va., October 26, 2026—C&F Financial Corporation (the Corporation) (NASDAQ: CFFI), the holding company for C&F Bank, today reported consolidated net income of $8.3 million for the third quarter of 2026, an increase of 16.1 percent, compared to $7.1 million for the third quarter of 2025. The Corporation reported consolidated net income of $23.7 million for the first nine months of 2026, an increase of 16.8 percent, compared to $20.3 million for the first nine months of 2025.

​

Included in net income for the first nine months of 2026 were the effects of the sale of an equity interest in Bearing Insurance Group, LLC (the “Bearing equity interest”), resulting in a pre-tax gain of $8.3 million, and a securities portfolio restructuring (the “Portfolio Restructuring”), which resulted in a pre-tax loss of $7.1 million.  Adjusted net income, a non-GAAP measure, increased $2.7 million, or 13.1 percent, for the first nine months of 2026, compared to the same period of 2025, which excludes the effects of the items mentioned above. Community Banking Segment.  The community banking segment reported net income of $8.9 million and $24.2 million for the third quarter and first nine months of 2026, respectively, compared to $7.4 million and $19.9 million for the same periods of 2025 due primarily to:

​

●higher interest income resulting from higher average balances of loans and securities and higher average interest rates on loans and securities, due primarily to an increase in market interest rates and the Portfolio Restructuring; and
●a pre-tax gain of $8.3 million on the sale of the Bearing equity interest in the first nine months of 2026, reported in investment income from other equity interests;

partially offset by:

●higher salaries and employee benefits due primarily to the addition of seasoned lending teams with the expansion into the Roanoke and Lynchburg Virginia markets in the third quarters of 2025 and 2026, respectively, annual compensation adjustments, and increased employee incentive accruals associated with improved financial performance;
●the Portfolio Restructuring, which resulted in a pre-tax loss of $7.1 million for the first nine months of 2026, reported in net loss on sales of available for sale securities;
●higher provision for credit losses for the first nine months of 2026 as compared to the same period of 2025 due primarily to the reversal of a specific reserve in the second quarter of 2025 upon the resolution of a nonperforming commercial real estate loan; and
●higher data processing expenses associated with investments in information technology infrastructure projects.

​

Adjusted net income for the community banking segment, which excludes the effects of the sale of the Bearing equity interest and the Portfolio Restructuring, was $23.5 million for the first nine months of 2026 compared to $19.9 million for the same period in 2025. Adjusted net income for the community banking segment increased $3.5 million for the first nine months of 2026 compared to the same period in 2025 due primarily to the other items discussed above.

​

Average loans increased $133.0 million, or 8.6 percent, for the third quarter of 2026 and $133.5 million, or 8.9 percent, for the first nine months of 2026 compared to the same periods of 2025 due primarily to growth in the commercial real estate and land acquisition and development segments of the loan portfolio. Average deposits increased $99.1 million, or

4.4 percent, for the third quarter of 2026 and $142.7 million, or 6.4 percent, for the first nine months of 2026 compared to the same periods of 2025.

​

Average interest-earning asset yields at the community banking segment were higher for the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to the same periods of 2025 due primarily to higher average interest rates on securities and loans and higher average balances of loans. In the Portfolio Restructuring, the community banking segment sold $72.6 million in book value of securities with a weighted average yield of 1.40% and representing approximately 14.7% of the entire securities portfolio, and purchased approximately $67.8 million of securities with a weighted average yield of 4.70%. Average loan yields increased for the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 at the community banking segment due primarily to higher average market interest rates. Average costs of interest-bearing deposits were lower for the third quarter and the first nine months of 2026 compared to the same periods of 2025 due primarily to a decrease in average interest rates paid on time deposits.  

​

The community banking segment’s nonaccrual loans were $1.1 million at both September 30, 2026 and December 31, 2025. The community banking segment recorded no provision for credit losses in the third quarter of 2026 compared to a net reversal of $100,000 in the third quarter of 2025. For the first nine months of 2026, the community banking segment recorded $450,000 of provision for credit losses compared to a net reversal of $300,000 for the first nine months of 2025. At September 30, 2026 the allowance for credit losses was $17.8 million compared to $17.4 million at December 31, 2025. The allowance for credit losses as a percentage of total loans decreased to 1.05 percent at September 30, 2026 from 1.10 percent at December 31, 2025 due primarily to changes in the forecast of key credit loss model assumptions, which includes the forecast of the national unemployment rate derived from the Federal Open Market Committee of the Federal Reserve Board. Management believes that the level of the allowance for credit losses is adequate to reflect the net amount expected to be collected.

Mortgage Banking Segment.  The mortgage banking segment reported net income of $266,000 for the third quarter of 2026 compared to $641,000 for the same period of 2025 due primarily to:

​

●lower gains on sales of loans due primarily to lower margins;

partially offset by:

●higher mortgage lender services fee income.

​

The mortgage banking segment reported net income of $2.2 million for the first nine months of 2026 compared to $2.1 million for the same period of 2025 due primarily to:

​

●higher gains on sales of loans and higher mortgage banking fee income due to higher volume of mortgage loan originations; and
●higher mortgage lender services fee income;

partially offset by:

●higher variable expenses tied to mortgage loan origination volume such as commissions and bonuses, reported in salaries and employee benefits, and higher loan processing and collection expenses.

​

Mortgage banking segment loan originations increased 3.4 percent and 18.6 percent for the third quarter and first nine months of 2026, respectively, compared to the same periods of 2025 as the mortgage interest rate environment was generally more favorable during the first nine months of 2026 compared to the first nine months of 2025. With interest rates rising in the third quarter of 2026, refinancings decreased during the quarter by 22.7 percent compared to the third quarter of 2025. Mortgage loan originations for the mortgage banking segment were $172.8 million for the third quarter of 2026, comprised of $158.3 million home purchases and $14.5 million refinancings, compared to $167.0 million for the third quarter of 2025, comprised of $148.2 million home purchases and $18.8 million refinancings. Mortgage loan originations for the mortgage banking segment were $586.1 million for the first nine months of 2026, comprised of $510.0 million home purchases and $76.1 million refinancings, compared to $494.3 million for the first nine months of 2025, comprised of $447.1 million home purchases and $47.2 million refinancings. Mortgage banking segment originations include originations of loans sold to the community banking segment, at prices similar to those paid by third-party investors. Intercompany gains, losses, and other income and expenses related to these transactions are eliminated to reach consolidated totals.

Through the Lender Solutions division of the mortgage banking segment, mortgage lender services fee income is derived from providing mortgage origination functions to third-party mortgage lenders for a fee. Mortgage lender services fee income increased to $907,000 and $2.7 million for the third quarter and first nine months of 2026, respectively, compared to $768,000 and $2.1 million for the same periods of 2025 due primarily to an increase in the number of third-party mortgage lenders serviced and increased mortgage loan volume in the industry.

​

During the third quarter and first nine months of 2026, the mortgage banking segment recorded net reversals of provision for indemnification losses of $35,000 and $95,000, respectively, compared to net reversals of provision for indemnification losses of $75,000 and $135,000 in the same periods of 2025. The allowance for indemnifications was $1.1 million and $1.2 million at September 30, 2026 and December 31, 2025, respectively. Management believes that the indemnification reserve is sufficient to absorb losses related to loans that have been sold in the secondary market.

Consumer Finance Segment.  The consumer finance segment reported net income of $303,000 for the third quarter of 2026 compared to $231,000 for the same period of 2025 due primarily to:

​

●lower provision for credit losses due primarily to a decrease in the balance of loans; and
●lower interest expense allocation on borrowings from the community banking segment as a result of lower average balance of borrowings and lower average interest rates;

partially offset by:

●lower interest income resulting from lower average balances of loans and lower loan yields.

​

The consumer finance segment reported net income of $760,000 for the first nine months of 2026 compared to $1.0 million for the same period of 2025 due primarily to:

​

●higher provision for credit losses due primarily to higher net charge-offs;

partially offset by:

●lower interest expense allocation on borrowings from the community banking segment as a result of lower average interest rates and lower average balance of borrowings.

 ​

Average loans decreased $10.0 million, or 2.2 percent, for the third quarter of 2026 and decreased $5.3 million, or 1.1 percent, for the first nine months of 2026, compared to the same periods in 2025 due primarily to a decrease in marine and recreational vehicle loans as the third party administrator of that program significantly decreased sales of those loans to outside parties during 2025, which led to the consumer finance segment ending future purchases under the program during the third quarter of 2025. The marine and recreational vehicle portfolio is expected to run off over time, subject to normal repayment activity and credit performance. The consumer finance segment experienced net charge-offs at an annualized rate of 2.61 percent of average total loans for the first nine months of 2026 compared to 2.51 percent for the first nine months of 2025 due primarily to a mix shift in the portfolio as balances of marine and recreational vehicle loans, which historically have had lower charge-off rates than the automobile portfolio, continued to decrease. At September 30, 2026, total delinquent loans as a percentage of total loans was 3.39 percent compared to 4.38 percent at December 31, 2025 and 4.00 percent at September 30, 2025.

​

The consumer finance segment, at times, offers payment deferrals as a portfolio management technique to achieve higher ultimate cash collections on select loan accounts. Average amounts of payment deferrals of automobile loans on a monthly basis, which are not included in delinquent loans, were 2.05 percent and 1.59 percent of average automobile loans outstanding during the third quarter and first nine months of 2026, respectively, compared to 1.88 percent and 1.79 percent during the same periods of 2025 and 1.40 percent during the second quarter of 2026.

​

The allowance for credit losses was $21.8 million, or 4.84 percent of total loans, at September 30, 2026 compared to $22.3 million, or 4.79 percent of total loans, at December 31, 2025. The increase in the percent of allowance to total loans from December 31, 2025 to September 30, 2026 is due primarily to a mix shift in the portfolio as balances of marine and recreational vehicle loans, which historically have had lower charge-off rates than the automobile portfolio, continued to decrease. Management believes that the level of the allowance for credit losses is adequate to reflect the net amount expected to be collected. If loan performance deteriorates resulting in further elevated delinquencies or net charge-offs, the provision for credit losses may increase in future periods.

Liquidity. The objective of the Corporation’s liquidity management is to ensure the continuous availability of funds to satisfy the credit needs of our customers and the demands of our depositors, creditors and investors. Uninsured deposits represent an estimate of amounts above the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) insurance coverage limit of $250,000. As of September 30, 2026, the Corporation’s uninsured deposits were approximately $720.4 million, or 30.6 percent of total deposits. Excluding intercompany cash holdings and municipal deposits, which are secured with pledged securities, amounts uninsured were approximately $587.2 million, or 24.9 percent of total deposits as of September 30, 2026. The Corporation’s liquid assets, which include cash and due from banks, interest-bearing deposits at other banks and nonpledged securities available for sale, were $389.4 million and borrowing availability was $641.7 million as of September 30, 2026, which in total exceed uninsured deposits, excluding intercompany cash holdings and secured municipal deposits, by $443.9 million as of September 30, 2026.

​

In addition to deposits, the Corporation utilizes short-term and long-term borrowings as sources of funds. Short-term borrowings from the Federal Reserve Bank and the Federal Home Loan Bank of Atlanta (FHLB) may be used to fund the Corporation’s day-to-day operations. Total borrowings increased to $138.1 million at September 30, 2026 from $113.3 million at December 31, 2025 due primarily to an increase in FHLB advances during the first nine months of 2026 that were used to help fund loan growth.

​

Additional sources of liquidity available to the Corporation include cash flows from operations, loan payments and payoffs, deposit growth, maturities, calls and sales of securities, the issuance of brokered certificates of deposit and the capacity to borrow additional funds.

Capital and Dividends.  During the third quarter of 2026, the Corporation declared a quarterly cash dividend of 48 cents per share. This dividend, which was paid to shareholders on October 1, 2026, represents a payout ratio of 18.8 percent of earnings per share for the third quarter of 2026. The Corporation continually reviews the amount of cash dividends per share and the resulting dividend payout ratio in light of changes in economic conditions, current and future capital levels and requirements, and expected future earnings.

​

Total consolidated equity increased $7.0 million at September 30, 2026 compared to December 31, 2025 due primarily to net income partially offset by higher unrealized losses in the market value of securities available for sale, which are recognized as a component of other comprehensive income, and by dividends paid on the Corporation’s common stock. The Corporation’s securities available for sale are fixed income debt securities and their unrealized loss position is a result of increased market interest rates since they were purchased. The Corporation expects to recover its investments in debt securities through scheduled payments of principal and interest. Unrealized losses are not expected to affect the earnings or regulatory capital of the Corporation or C&F Bank. The accumulated other comprehensive loss related to the Corporation’s securities available for sale, net of deferred income taxes, increased to $23.2 million at September 30, 2026 compared to $10.2 million at December 31, 2025 due primarily to an increase in debt security market interest rates.

​

As of September 30, 2026, C&F Bank was categorized as well capitalized under the FDIC’s regulatory framework for prompt corrective action. To be categorized as well capitalized under regulations applicable at September 30, 2026, C&F Bank was required to maintain minimum total risk-based, Tier 1 risk-based, CET1 risk-based and Tier 1 leverage ratios. In addition to the regulatory risk-based capital requirements, C&F Bank must maintain a capital conservation buffer of additional capital of 2.5 percent of risk-weighted assets as required by the Basel III capital rules.  The Corporation and C&F Bank exceeded these ratios at September 30, 2026. For additional information, see “Capital Ratios” below.  The above-mentioned ratios are not impacted by unrealized losses on securities available for sale. In the event that all of these unrealized losses become realized into earnings, the Corporation and C&F Bank would both continue to exceed minimum capital requirements, including the capital conservation buffer, and be considered well capitalized.
