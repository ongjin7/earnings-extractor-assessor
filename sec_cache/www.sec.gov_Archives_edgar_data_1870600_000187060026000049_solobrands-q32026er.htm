Solo Brands, Inc. Announces Fiscal 2026 Third Quarter Results
Positive Operating Cash Flow and Improved Segment Profitability Reflect Disciplined Execution
New Product Innovation Drives Consumer Engagement Across Channels
Grapevine, Texas, November 12, 2026: Solo Brands, Inc. (OTCQB: SBDS) (“Solo Brands” or “the Company”), a premium outdoor lifestyle company that develops and markets branded products across its Solo Stove, Chubbies, and Watersports segments, today announced its financial results for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026.
John Larson, President and Chief Executive Officer, commented, “While the third quarter is typically our seasonally smallest sales quarter, we continued to make measurable progress strengthening the business. We improved segment profitability, generated more than $6 million of operating cash flow, and further advanced our transformation strategy through disciplined execution, cost management, and operational efficiency initiatives. These results reflect our continued focus on building a more resilient, leaner cash-generative company.
“While revenue performance remains below our expectations, we are encouraged by improving consumer engagement and the strong response to recent product innovation. Products launched over the past year for our Solo Stove segment represented nearly half of our website sales during the quarter, highlighting the strength of our innovation pipeline and the value of our brand portfolio. We are also expanding how we engage consumers through influencer-led marketing, social commerce initiatives, and platforms such as TikTok Shop, helping us reach new audiences and build deeper customer connections. As we enter holiday 2026 with a leaner inventory position, strong liquidity, and growing momentum in our newest products, we remain focused on driving sustainable growth, profitability, and long-term shareholder value.”
Consolidated Third Quarter 2026 Highlights Compared to Third Quarter 2025
•Net sales of $43.7 million decreased 17.6% from $53.0 million, primarily the result of the decrease in net sales across our reportable segments.
•Gross profit of $27.7 million, or 63.4% of net sales, compared to $31.8 million, or 60.0% of net sales. Adjusted gross profit(1) of $28.2 million, or 64.4% of net sales, compared to $32.2 million, or 60.6% of net sales. Gross profit was impacted by lower net sales and changes in channel mix, partially offset by reduced promotional activity and tariff impacts compared to the prior year period.
•Operating expenses of $42.1 million decreased 12.3% from $48.0 million.
•Selling, general & administrative expenses of $31.6 million decreased 20.0% from $39.5 million, primarily due to lower payroll and related costs, reduced marketing spend within the Solo Stove segment, and lower variable costs associated with lower DTC net sales, reflecting disciplined expense management across the business.
•Restructuring, contract termination and impairment charges were $6.0 million compared to $1.9 million, primarily reflecting non-cash impairments and one-time costs from streamlining our fulfillment footprint and exiting underperforming operations.
•Net loss attributable to Solo Brands, Inc. of $20.8 million, or $8.08 diluted loss per share of Class A common stock(2), compared to $15.0 million, or $9.22 diluted loss per share of Class A common stock(2).
•Adjusted net loss attributable to Solo Brands, Inc.(1) of $11.6 million, or $4.51 adjusted diluted loss per share of Class A common stock(1)(2), compared to $7.1 million, or $4.33 adjusted diluted loss per share of Class A common stock(1)(2).
•Adjusted EBITDA(1) of $(2.1) million, or (4.9)% of net sales, improved from $(5.1) million, or (9.6)% of net sales.
Segment Third Quarter 2026 Highlights Compared to Third Quarter 2025
Solo Stove
•Net sales of $24.7 million decreased 19.9%, reflecting lower unit volumes in core fire pits partially mitigated by new product introductions, as well as softness in the retail channel.
•Segment adjusted EBITDA of $2.3 million, or 9.4% of net sales, compared to $1.4 million, or 4.4% of net sales, reflecting the benefit of ongoing cost reduction initiatives, including leaner operations, payroll, and marketing, amid lower net sales.
Chubbies
•Net sales of $13.7 million decreased 16.8%, reflecting variability in customer demand within both the DTC and retail channels.
•Segment adjusted EBITDA of $0.2 million, or 1.2% of net sales, compared to $(1.2) million, or (7.5)% of net sales, primarily reflecting the benefit of cost reduction initiatives including leaner operations and payroll, partially offset by lower gross profit on the decrease in net sales.
Watersports
•Net sales of $5.3 million decreased 7.4%, reflecting softer sales on less promotional activity within the DTC channel, partially offset by increased customer demand within the retail channel.
•Segment adjusted EBITDA of $0.5 million, or 8.9% of net sales, compared to $0.5 million, or 8.3% of net sales.

Consolidated Nine Months Ended September 30, 2026 Highlights Compared to Nine Months Ended September 30, 2025
•Net sales of $195.1 million decreased 12.4% from $222.5 million, primarily the result of the decrease in both DTC and retail channel net sales within the Solo Stove segment, and, to a lesser extent, declines in both channels within the Chubbies segment, partially offset by an increase in retail channel sales within our Watersports segment.
•Gross profit of $113.6 million, or 58.3% of net sales, compared to $131.1 million, or 58.9% of net sales. Adjusted gross profit(1) of $116.0 million, or 59.5% of net sales, compared to $131.9 million, or 59.3% of net sales. •Operating expenses of $129.3 million decreased 22.9% from $167.7 million.
•Selling, general & administrative expenses of $107.4 million decreased 14.9% from $126.2 million, primarily due to lower payroll and related costs, reduced marketing spend within the Solo Stove segment, and lower variable costs associated with lower DTC net sales, reflecting disciplined expense management across the business.
•Restructuring, contract termination and impairment charges were $8.2 million compared to $18.0 million, as lower consulting, retention, and severance costs were partially offset by non-cash impairments from closing distribution centers, Oru’s manufacturing facility, and an underperforming retail store operation.
•Net loss attributable to Solo Brands, Inc. of $30.6 million, or $12.03 diluted loss per share of Class A common stock(2), compared to $40.7 million, or $26.59 diluted loss per share of Class A common stock(2).
•Adjusted net loss attributable to Solo Brands, Inc.(1) of $15.2 million, or $5.96 adjusted diluted loss per share of Class A common stock(1)(2), compared to $11.8 million, or $7.71 adjusted diluted loss per share of Class A common stock(1)(2).
•Adjusted EBITDA(1) of $13.0 million, or 6.6% of net sales, improved from $8.9 million, or 4.0% of net sales.
Segment Nine Months Ended September 30, 2026 Highlights Compared to Nine Months Ended September 30, 2025
Solo Stove
•Net sales of $73.4 million decreased 22.9%, reflecting lower unit volumes in core fire pits partially mitigated by new product introductions, as well as softness in the retail channel.
•Segment adjusted EBITDA of $4.3 million, or 5.9% of net sales, compared to $3.3 million, or 3.4% of net sales, primarily reflecting the benefit of ongoing cost reduction initiatives, including leaner operations, payroll, and marketing, amid lower net sales and, to a lesser extent, reduced promotional activity compared to the prior year period.
Chubbies
•Net sales of $91.0 million decreased 12.2%, reflecting variability in customer demand within both the DTC and retail channels.
•Segment adjusted EBITDA of $20.2 million, or 22.2% of net sales, compared to $21.5 million, or 20.8% of net sales, reflecting lower gross profit on the decrease in net sales, partially offset by the benefit of cost reduction initiatives, including leaner operations and payroll.
Watersports
•Net sales of $30.6 million increased 32.4%, reflecting an expanded partnership with a key customer within the retail channel, partially offset by softer sales on less promotional activity within the DTC channel.
•Segment adjusted EBITDA of $5.3 million, or 17.4% of net sales, compared to $2.8 million, or 12.3% of net sales, reflecting the higher net sales during the period and the benefit of cost reduction initiatives, specifically payroll and marketing.

About Solo Brands, Inc.
Solo Brands is a premium outdoor lifestyle company that develops and markets branded products designed to enhance outdoor recreation, entertainment, and adventure while bringing people together. The Company operates across three primary outdoor lifestyle categories – Solo Stove, known for its highly rated fire pits, griddles, coolers, and accessories; Chubbies, a lifestyle brand focused on casual wear, activewear, and swimwear; and Watersports, which includes ISLE, a maker of inflatable and hard paddle boards and related accessories, and Oru Kayak, the innovator of origami-inspired folding kayaks. Headquartered in Grapevine, Texas, Solo Brands employs approximately 300 associates. Its products are sold through leading retailer partners, direct-to-consumer channels, and a growing network of distributors worldwide. For a listing of our brands and more information, please visit https://solobrands.com.

Non-GAAP Financial Measures
We report our financial results in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States (“U.S. GAAP”); however, management believes that certain non-GAAP financial measures provide users of our financial information with useful supplemental information that enables a better comparison of our performance across periods. We use adjusted gross profit, adjusted gross profit margin, adjusted net income, adjusted net income (loss) per Class A common stock, adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA margin as non-GAAP financial measures, because we believe they are useful indicators of our operating performance. Our management uses these non-GAAP measures principally as measures of our operating performance and believes that these non-GAAP measures are useful to our investors because they are frequently used by securities analysts, investors and other interested parties in their evaluation of the operating performance of companies in industries similar to ours. Our management also uses these non-GAAP measures for planning purposes, including the preparation of our annual operating budget and financial projections.
None of these non-GAAP measures is a measurement of financial performance under U.S. GAAP. These non-GAAP measures should not be considered in isolation or as a substitute for a measure of our liquidity or operating performance prepared in accordance with U.S. GAAP and are not indicative of net income (loss) as determined under U.S. GAAP. In addition, the exclusion of certain gains or losses in the calculation of non-GAAP financial measures should not be construed as an inference that these items are unusual or infrequent as they may recur in the future, nor should it be construed that our future results will be unaffected by unusual or non-recurring items. These non-GAAP financial measures have limitations that should be considered before using these measures to evaluate our liquidity or financial performance. Some of these limitations are as follows.
These non-GAAP measures exclude certain tax payments that may require a reduction in cash available to us; do not reflect our cash expenditures, or future requirements, for capital expenditures (including capitalized software development costs) or contractual commitments; do not reflect changes in, or cash requirements for, our working capital needs; do not reflect the cash requirements necessary to service interest or principal payments on our debt; exclude certain purchase accounting adjustments related to acquisitions; and exclude equity-based compensation expense, which has recently been, and will continue to be for the foreseeable future, a significant recurring expense for our business and an important part of our compensation strategy.
In addition, other companies may define and calculate similarly-titled non-GAAP financial measures differently than us, thereby limiting the usefulness of these non-GAAP financial measures as a comparative tool. Because of these and other limitations, you should consider our non-GAAP measures only as supplemental to other U.S. GAAP-based financial performance measures.
Adjusted Net Income (Loss)
We calculate adjusted net income (loss) as net income (loss) excluding restructuring, contract termination and impairment charges and other costs that are believed by management to be non-operating in nature and not representative of the Company’s core operating performance, as listed below under “Non-GAAP Adjustments”. Adjusted net income (loss) attributable to noncontrolling interests is calculated as income (loss) before income taxes, adjusted in the same manner as adjusted net income (loss), adjusted for the allocable attribution to the noncontrolling interest (when applicable).
Adjusted Net Income (Loss) per Share of Class A Common Stock
We calculate adjusted net income (loss) per share of Class A common stock as adjusted net income (loss), as defined above, less the allocable portion of net income to the noncontrolling interest, divided by weighted average diluted shares or weighted average shares of Class A common stock, respectively, as calculated under U.S. GAAP.
EBITDA
We calculate EBITDA as net income (loss) before interest expense, income taxes, and depreciation and amortization expenses.
Adjusted EBITDA
We calculate adjusted EBITDA as net income (loss) before interest expense, income taxes, depreciation and amortization expenses, restructuring, contract termination and impairment charges, equity-based compensation expense, and other costs that are believed by management to be non-operating in nature and not representative of the Company’s core operating performance, as listed below under “Non-GAAP Adjustments”.
Adjusted EBITDA Margin
We calculate adjusted EBITDA margin as adjusted EBITDA, divided by net sales.
Adjusted Gross Profit
We calculate adjusted gross profit as gross profit, plus inventory charges associated with restructuring and consolidation activities, inventory fair value write-ups and depreciation and amortization recognized within cost of goods sold.
Adjusted Gross Profit Margin
We calculate adjusted gross profit margin as adjusted gross profit, divided by net sales.

Non-GAAP Adjustments
In addition to the costs specifically noted under the non-GAAP metrics above, the Company believes that evaluation of its financial performance can be enhanced by a supplemental presentation of results that exclude costs believed by management to be non-operating in nature and not representative of the Company’s core operating performance. These costs are excluded in order to provide a basis for evaluating operating results in future periods.
•Inventory charges associated with restructuring and consolidation activities - primarily represents the inventory writedown of raw materials related to the closure of the Oru manufacturing facility, which was the Company’s only remaining manufacturing operation.
•Restructuring, contract termination, impairment and related charges - for 2026, represents charges related to impairment of long-lived assets, charges related to cost saving initiatives, such as reduction in force, closure of distribution centers, retention payments to key personnel, closure and impairment of a manufacturing facility, as well as costs related to the engagement of strategic consulting firms for operational planning, legal entity reorganizations, and additional cost saving initiative identification. For 2025, represents charges related to impairment of long-lived assets, cost saving initiatives, such as the reduction in force, closure of distribution centers, owned retail store lease terminations, impairments and modifications, termination of underperforming licensing arrangements and other contracts, retention payments to key personnel, as well as costs related to the engagement of strategic consulting firms for operational planning, legal entity reorganizations, additional cost saving initiative identification and internal management reporting optimization.
•Amortization expense - represents the non-cash amortization of the following:
•intangible assets related to the reorganization transactions and acquisitions, and additions to patents in regard to their defense;
•website development costs; and
•capitalized software.
•Depreciation expense - represents the non-cash depreciation of property and equipment.
•Business optimization and expansion expenses - represents costs related to the engagement of strategic consulting firms for business optimization and expansion planning, and the transaction with the former sellers of TerraFlame in 2025.
•Costs associated with the refinancing amendment - represents costs related to the engagement of strategic consulting firms in conjunction with the 2025 debt refinancing.
•Equity-based compensation expense - represents the non-cash expense related to the incentive units, restricted stock units, options, performance stock units, special performance stock units, executive performance stock units and employee stock purchases, with vesting occurring over time and settled with the Company’s Class A common stock. Forfeitures are recognized in the period incurred and reflected as a reduction of the non-cash expense previously recognized for awards not yet vested.
•Transaction costs - represents costs for professional service fees incurred in connection with potential and completed registered securities offerings, merger, acquisition and/or divestiture activities, and tariff refund specialists.
•Changes in fair value of contingent earn-out liability - represents the charge to mark the contingent earn-out consideration to fair value in connection with the prior period acquisitions.
•Management transition costs - represents costs primarily related to executive transition costs for executive search fees and related costs for the transition of certain members of management, such as severance costs.
•Tax impact of adjusting items - for 2026, reflects no tax effect because of the impacts of the Corporate Simplification and valuation allowance. For 2025, represents the tax impact of the respective adjustments for each non-GAAP financial measure calculated at an expected statutory rate of 21.0%, adjusted to reflect the allocation to the controlling interest.
•Prior period tax refund received - represents a tax refund received and recorded in the current period related to a prior period.
•Reversal of valuation allowance - represents the removal of the valuation allowance recorded within the period, as determined through revision of the current period tax provision to reflect the non-GAAP adjustments to income (loss) before income taxes.
