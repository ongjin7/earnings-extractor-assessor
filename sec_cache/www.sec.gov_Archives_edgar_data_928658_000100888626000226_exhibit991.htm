COVENANT LOGISTICS GROUP ANNOUNCES Third QUARTER 2026
 
FINANCIAL AND OPERATING RESULTS
 
CHATTANOOGA, TENNESSEE – October 29, 2026 - Covenant Logistics Group, Inc. (NYSE: CVLG) (“Covenant” or the “Company”) announced today financial and operating results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026. The Company’s conference call to discuss the quarter will be held at 10:00 A.M. Eastern Time on Friday, October 30, 2026.
 
Chairman and Chief Executive Officer David R. Parker commented, “We were pleased with our third-quarter earnings of $0.40 per diluted share, or $0.50 per diluted share on a non-GAAP adjusted basis. This marked the first time since the second quarter of 2022 that adjusted earnings per diluted share increased both sequentially and year over year.
 
“The third quarter reflected strong coordination between our Truckload and Managed Freight segments as we continued to re-allocate assets to customers that are seeking long-term capacity for their needs and are willing to offer a reasonable return on our meaningful investments in professional drivers, new equipment, and advanced technology. Exiting the third quarter, approximately 80% of our fleet assets were under long-term contracts, and we continue to receive inbound demand from our customers on growing our committed capacity.  I’m proud of our team’s execution, as repositioning hundreds of drivers, tractors, and trailers, and starting up several new operational and administrative systems during the quarter was a big accomplishment. Repositioning for new contracts created some disruption to utilization and added cost to the Truckload fleets. This was more than countered by higher revenue and operating income in Managed Freight, as our historical brokerage operations successfully sourced capacity for overflow Expedited freight, and the brokerage operations we acquired in the fourth quarter of 2025 had their best quarter as part of Covenant. Margin improvement reflected customer rate increases implemented during the first half of the year and lower purchased transportation costs as spot rates eased during the quarter. These improvements followed three consecutive quarters of margin compression.
 
“From a market perspective, freight demand is adequate in most sectors and strong in specific sectors that are aligned with U.S. economic growth.  Driver capacity is tight, and the cost of recruiting, retaining, and compensating drivers is increasing significantly.  Cost inflation is weighing on nearly every significant line item in our business, including wages and benefits, equipment ownership and maintenance, and technology. In this environment, rate increases have been largely offset by cost increases. However, we expect sequential improvement to continue in the fourth quarter as a higher percentage of our fleet operates under committed arrangements and we incur less repositioning cost.
 
“Our goal is to place substantially all of our asset-based business under long-term dedicated or other committed contracts by the end of this freight market upcycle. Consistent with our strategy, which reduced volatility during the recent freight market downturn, we intend to remain disciplined and patient in pursuing customers and markets that support sustainable long-term value creation.
 
“Our 49% equity method investment with Transport Enterprise Leasing (“TEL”) exceeded our expectations for a second consecutive quarter by contributing pre-tax net income of $5.7 million, or $0.16 per share, compared to $3.6 million, or $0.10 per share, in the 2025 quarter.  TEL’s results benefited from the combination of equipment leasing growth, as well as higher equipment sale gains.”

Combined Truckload Revenue
 
Paul Bunn, the Company’s President, commented on Combined Truckload operations, “For the quarter, total revenue in our Truckload operations increased 2.5%, to $204.6 million.  The increase in total revenue consisted of $10.8 million more fuel surcharge revenue, which varies with the cost of fuel, offset by $5.8 million less freight revenue. The reduction in freight revenue is largely attributable to a 8.9% decrease in the average fleet size, offset with the improvements to pricing and freight mix discussed earlier.  
 
Expedited Truckload Revenue
 
Mr. Bunn added, “Freight revenue in our Expedited segment decreased $9.6 million, or 12.0%. Average total tractors decreased by 184 units or 21.4% to 677, compared to 861 in the prior year quarter. Average freight revenue per tractor per week increased 12.0% compared to the prior year. During the quarter, our Expedited fleet continued to transition to multi-year committed capacity agreements. By the end of the quarter, approximately 58% of our Expedited tractors were serving under committed capacity agreements, compared to approximately 40% three months ago.  As we progress throughout this cycle, our focus for our Expedited fleet is to serve customers who truly need our teams and are willing to agree to multi-year agreements as a sign of their commitment.
 
Dedicated Truckload Revenue
 
“For the quarter, freight revenue in our Dedicated segment increased $3.8 million, or 4.2%. Average total tractors decreased by 30 units or 1.9% to 1,509, compared to 1,539 in the prior year quarter. Average freight revenue per tractor per week increased 6.2% as a result of the expansion of our agricultural protein-related business and exiting non-specialized dedicated business that has struggled to meet profitability thresholds.”
 
Combined Truckload Operating Expenses
 
Mr. Bunn continued, “Truckload freight revenue per total mile increased approximately 19.7% year-over-year, and Truckload operating expenses increased approximately 20.8% year-over-year, each on a non-GAAP adjusted basis. Elevated expenses during the quarter reflected a combination of ongoing inflation (wages, benefits, equipment depreciation and maintenance, and technology) and quarter-specific costs (equipment repositioning, driver hiring in new locations, costs and lower utilization associated with start-ups for a large number of contracts, and the effects of extreme heat on certain Dedicated operations). As mix continues to shift toward more committed capacity contracts, we expect the repositioning and start-up costs to moderate as a percentage of revenue and for ongoing rate increases to more than outpace general inflation.

Capitalization, Liquidity and Capital Expenditures
 
Tripp Grant, the Company’s Chief Financial Officer, added the following comments: “At September 30, 2026, our total indebtedness, comprised of total debt and finance lease obligations, net of cash (“net indebtedness”), decreased by $12.4 million to approximately $283.9 million as compared to December 31, 2025. In addition, our net indebtedness to total capitalization improved to 40.2% at September 30, 2026, from 42.3% at December 31, 2025.
 
“At September 30, 2026, we had cash and cash equivalents totaling $5.1 million. Under our ABL credit facility, we had $35.0 million in outstanding borrowings, undrawn letters of credit outstanding of $19.8 million, and immediate available borrowing capacity of $75.2 million.
 
“At the end of the quarter, we had $0.2 million in assets held for sale that we anticipate disposing of within twelve months. The average age of our tractors increased to 26 months compared to 23 months a year ago. Given the mix change between our high mileage expedited fleet and lower mileage dedicated fleets, going forward, we anticipate the average age of our tractors to range from 25 to 28 months.
  
“Our net capital expenditures for the first three quarters of the year were approximately $18.4 million, as proceeds from fleet downsizing and selling excess used equipment kept pace with the investment in new replacement equipment. For the balance of 2026, our expectations for net capital equipment expenditures range from $35.0 million to $40.0 million.”
 
Outlook
 
Mr. Parker concluded, “Based on our growing customer pipeline, we expect Dedicated fleet growth and a stable Expedited fleet with a higher proportion of capacity under dedicated or committed contracts. We are also focused on reducing overhead and other controllable costs as a percentage of revenue, which we expect will support gradual margin expansion. For the fourth quarter of 2026, we expect a modest sequential increase in earnings per share, with anticipated operating margin improvement from our asset-based operations partially offset by lower income from our equity method investment, TEL. Longer term, we believe our strategy can materially improve Combined Truckload operating margins through continued world-class customer service and disciplined asset reallocation to operations offering higher margins and returns.”

About Covenant Logistics Group
Covenant Logistics Group, Inc., through its subsidiaries, offers a portfolio of transportation and logistics services to customers throughout the United States. Primary services include asset-based expedited and dedicated truckload capacity, as well as asset-light warehousing, transportation management, and freight brokerage capability. In addition, Transport Enterprise Leasing is an affiliated company providing revenue equipment sales and leasing services to the trucking industry. Covenant's Class A common stock is traded on the New York Stock Exchange under the symbol, “CVLG.”
